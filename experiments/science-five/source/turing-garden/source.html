Open the field notes — why does anything grow?

This is a discrete Gray–Scott reaction-diffusion model. Two imaginary chemicals follow local rules: A feeds in, B is removed, and A + 2B → 3B. Diffusion spreads them at different rates. Shapes emerge without an artist specifying their final outline.

A′ = A + ∇²A − AB² + f(1−A)
B′ = B + 0.5∇²B + AB² − (f+k)B

The model uses forward Euler with Δt = 1 and a 3×3 Laplacian: center −1, four axial neighbors 0.2, four diagonal neighbors 0.05. Edges wrap into a torus. Concentrations are bounded to [0,1]; any numerical clipping is reported. These are dimensionless simulation steps, not seconds or a literal organism.

Try this: pause, plant a dot beside an existing front, then advance 20 steps at a time. Change only the feed rate. When does a membrane split, spread or vanish? Pattern names describe typical behavior, not guaranteed final shapes. A blank field can be a valid outcome.

The initial study is warmed for 900 steps. Restart reproduces that seed; drawing and slider changes then create a different history. Save state stores exact little-endian Float32 grids and parameters; restoration pauses at the saved step. Live behavior remains local to your browser.
